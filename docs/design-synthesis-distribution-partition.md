# Design synthesis feeder-aware panel partitioning

Overall review class: **Screening only**.

Design synthesis starts with the supplied maximum panel kVA and load-count limits. It also checks whether each generated feeder can be served by the existing sourced conductor catalog and a screened protective-device candidate. When a feeder cannot meet those constraints, the engine retries a deterministic bisection of that load group under the same original criteria. This applies to supported motor and nonmotor loads. Each trial runs route selection, conductor derating and terminal checks, voltage-drop screening, and device selection before another split is attempted. Already feasible nonmotor designs keep their existing output shape without partition metadata.

The engine first performs bounded design-only trials, then runs the existing operating-load, motor-starting, acceleration, and retained-current revision flow once using the selected panel memberships. The retry budget is therefore not reset for each partition. Existing panel identity and location-override rules remain in force: the identity planner retains a prior panel ID for its best-overlap descendant and allocates new IDs for other descendants. A location override remains attached to the descendant that retains its panel ID.

`proposal.distributionPartitionValidation` records the original panel count, final group memberships, unchanged kVA and load-count criteria, trial and split counts, split reasons, selected feeder conductor ampacities, device-selection outcomes, and the stopping reason. A feeder trial must have a feasible route decision, positive route thermal ampacity above its design floor, an evaluated in-limit voltage drop, a sized conductor meeting its floor, and a selected device. The search is bounded by the number of supplied loads and uses deterministic bisection; it is not a global optimization. If an individual load cannot be served, the proposal remains blocked with `DISTRIBUTION_GROUP_UNSERVABLE`. Identity allocation failure or incomplete trial membership blocks with `DISTRIBUTION_PARTITION_IDENTITY_BLOCKED`. If a later operating-study revision leaves a feeder below its retained route, ampacity, voltage-drop, or device-feasibility boundary, the proposal is blocked with `DISTRIBUTION_FINAL_REVISION_INFEASIBLE` and does not claim the earlier partition remains feasible.

Feeder motor design ampacity is passed directly to route and conductor sizing, independently of protective-device catalog selection. A missing device candidate therefore cannot reduce the conductor floor to zero. A returned feeder is marked `screening-sized` only when a supported conductor satisfies the computed floor and the applicable route, terminal, thermal, and voltage-drop constraints.

This check does not establish complete motor protection, short-circuit coordination, conductor protection approval, panelboard rating, installation suitability, or authority approval. Feeder device selections remain screening candidates. The algorithm does not relax criteria, add parallel conductors, or invent catalog data. Feasibility also depends on the current catalog and modeled route; an individually unsupported load blocks the proposal.

The retained synthetic cases use the original 500 kVA panel ceiling and 50-load count ceiling. The 25-motor case now forms two panels (13/12 loads), and the 50-motor case forms five (12/12/12/11/3). Their supplied 14 A motor full-load currents produce feeder design floors of 17.5 A per motor. Operating and initial DOL starting screens pass; complete motor protection remains unresolved and Apply stays disabled. These results close the default-grouping defects recorded in the earlier source-profile history milestone; they do not depend on the manually restricted eight-load examples.

The same 50-load layout with supported nonmotor 9 kW loads forms three panels (24/23/3). Its feeder floor uses the supplied electrical input power and power factor. Canonical project integration retains normalized lowercase record IDs, while generated identities, panel membership, and location overrides remain reconciled through existing storage APIs. Feasible cases at higher route temperature can now split into smaller groups; prohibited corridors remain excluded, and disconnected networks still block. Neither successful splitting nor a passing operating study proves minimum cost, a minimum panel count, or a fully qualified electrical design.

Generated loads also carry canonical `panelId`, breaker start and pole count. Each generated panel includes nonoverlapping circuit positions, breaker details linked to its branch cable, the known feeder source, and the screened candidate main rating. Circuit capacity starts at the existing 42-position display default and grows to fit supported assignments within the 512-position software bound. For example, 24 three-pole branches require 72 positions. These are proposed schedule positions, not a claim that a particular physical panel assembly is qualified or commercially available. Unsupported pole combinations, incomplete membership and exhausted circuit capacity block the projection. Main and branch ratings retain candidate status; assembly SCCR remains unevaluated.

Panel Schedule must display the generated assigned loads and connected totals without first adding default layout fields. This makes downstream rendering idempotent and avoids treating automatic page initialization as a manual edit during regeneration. Actual manual record or diagram edits retain the existing conflict protection.

Canonical panels own the complete breaker layout; their equipment mirrors retain shared identity and settings without duplicating every circuit slot. Saved route results retain geometry and selected routing decisions. Full thermal assessments and installation-coverage segments remain on the corresponding canonical cable records, while route exploration is summarized by its count. Complete proposal exports retain the original evidence. This avoids duplicate storage while keeping the existing 5 MiB project acceptance bound, named snapshots and undo behavior.

Verification records and exported examples are retained in `output/design-synthesis-distribution-partition-2026-10-23/`. Complete source-profile histories remain report/session evidence; this change does not introduce full-history canonical persistence. A compact history contract and the motor-containing canonical integration journey remain separate unfinished work.
