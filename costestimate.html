Project Cost Estimator

Method & Pricing Basis

Material costs are estimated from unit prices ($/ft for cable, tray, conduit) multiplied by quantities from the project schedules. Cable lengths come from routing results if available, otherwise from the cable schedule.

Labor costs use built-in conceptual productivity allowances:

  • Cable pull: 150 ft/hr → $75/hr = $0.50/ft installed
  • Tray installation: 30 ft/hr → $90/hr = $3.00/ft installed
  • Conduit installation: 25 ft/hr → $85/hr = $3.40/ft installed

Default unit prices are built-in 2024 USD conceptual allowances, not a licensed cost-book extract. Import a current supplier, internal, or licensed cost source for issued estimates.

This estimate does not include: engineering fees, permits, switchgear, transformers, or conduit fittings beyond a default unit cost per fitting.

Estimate Basis & Escalation

Normalize a pricing book to the project region and estimate date. Index values are stored with the estimate so a later reviewer can reproduce the adjustment without a live data connection.

Regional Labor Basis

Regional factor = local wage ÷ national wage. The national reference is the BLS OEWS May 2024 median annual electrician wage ($62,350 ÷ 2,080 hr). Enter the comparable local OEWS value; this factor scales the loaded contractor labor rates, not the wage itself.

Cost Estimator Help

This tool estimates project material and labor costs from your cable, tray, and conduit schedules.

For best results:

  • Run Optimal Route first — cable lengths from routing are used when available.
  • Enter tray lengths in the Raceway Schedule.
  • Override unit prices in the Price Overrides section to match your region.

Regional labor: Enter a comparable local BLS OEWS electrician wage. The tool scales the loaded contractor labor rate by local ÷ national wage; it does not substitute the employee wage for a contractor bill rate.

Escalation: Enter base and current values from the same BLS PPI material series and the same non-seasonally adjusted BLS ECI labor series. A value pair of 100/100 leaves that cost component unchanged.

Contingency: A percentage added to the grand total to cover unknowns. 15% is typical for early estimates.
